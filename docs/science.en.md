# The science behind Unslouch breaks

This page explains why the app does what it does and which research stands behind it.
For each part of the body: why it matters, what the app does, what studies show, and when
to see a doctor.

## How we chose

- We relied on systematic reviews, meta-analyses, randomised trials and guidance from
  professional bodies (WHO, American Academy of Ophthalmology, OSHA). Where nothing better
  exists we cite small individual studies and say so.
- The evidence is uneven. There is a lot of data on the benefits of movement and the harm of
  long sitting. There is little data on how often to take breaks or which exercises work best:
  studies are small, short and often have no control group. We point this out where it applies.
- We write "reduces fatigue" and "reduces discomfort", never "treats" or "prevents". The app
  helps you avoid sitting too long. It does not treat any disease.
- Figures were checked against abstracts and full texts. Where a number comes from a secondary
  source and we have not seen it in the original, we say so.

**This is not medical advice.** If something hurts, does not go away or gets in the way of your
life, see a doctor. Do the exercises without pain: if it hurts, stop.

## Eyes

### Why

When we look at a screen we blink two to three times less often: about 5-7 times a minute instead
of the usual 15. Many blinks are incomplete, the lid does not close fully. The tear film dries out,
which causes dryness, burning and a gritty feeling. The second source of fatigue is the muscle that
focuses the eye up close: it works for hours without rest. Looking into the distance lets it relax.

### What the app does

- Every 20 minutes it suggests looking into the distance, more than 6 metres (20 feet) away,
  for 20 seconds.
- Some breaks include a lid exercise: slowly close your eyes, gently squeeze the lids, open.
- Every 5 minutes the screen edges darken for a second as a cue to blink. No windows, no buttons.

### What studies show

- **American Academy of Ophthalmology (AAO).** People blink 5-7 times a minute at screens instead
  of about 15. Recommends looking into the distance regularly, artificial tears for dryness, screen
  at about arm's length. The exact rule, every 20 minutes looking 20 seconds at something more than
  6 metres (20 feet) away, is in the AAO article on blue-light glasses, which also says the Academy
  does not recommend special eyewear for computer use.
  https://www.aao.org/eye-health/tips-prevention/computer-usage ; https://www.aao.org/eye-health/tips-prevention/are-computer-glasses-worth-it
- **American Optometric Association (AOA).** Screen 50-70 cm away, centre of the screen 15-20°
  below eye level, breaks, vision correction when needed.
  https://www.aoa.org/healthy-eyes/eye-and-vision-conditions/computer-vision-syndrome
- **Sheppard & Wolffsohn, 2018, BMJ Open Ophthalmology 3:e000146.** Review: digital eye strain
  affects roughly half of computer users or more. Breaks, proper vision correction and dry eye
  care help. doi:10.1136/bmjophth-2018-000146
- **Talens-Estarelles et al., 2023, Contact Lens & Anterior Eye 46(2):101744.** 29 symptomatic
  users ran reminder software for distance breaks for two weeks. Symptoms of digital eye strain
  and dry eye went down, objective signs barely changed. Small study, no control group.
  doi:10.1016/j.clae.2022.101744
- **Kim et al., 2021, Contact Lens & Anterior Eye 44(3):101329.** 41 people with dry eye did a short
  blinking exercise every 20 minutes for four weeks. Symptoms went down and incomplete blinks fell
  from 54% to 34%. No control group. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32409236/
- **Wolffsohn et al., 2025, Contact Lens & Anterior Eye 48(5):102453.**
  15 repetitions three times a day for two weeks reduced symptoms and incomplete blinks.
  doi:10.1016/j.clae.2025.102453
- **Xu et al., 2025, npj Digital Medicine 8:703.** Randomised trial of app-based blink training, 40 people,
  30 days. Small study. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-02053-8
- **Singh et al., 2023, Cochrane Review CD013244.pub2.** Blue-light filtering lenses may not
  reduce eye strain from screens: 17 randomised trials, 619 participants, low certainty.
  That is why we do not recommend them. doi:10.1002/14651858.CD013244.pub2

### When to see a doctor

- Symptoms persist even though you take breaks.
- Double vision.
- Eye pain or marked redness.
- Sudden loss of vision, even brief: this is urgent.
- After 40, reading up close has become hard: you probably need reading glasses, an
  ophthalmologist or optometrist can prescribe them.

## Neck and shoulders

### Why

At a screen, the upper trapezius holds the shoulders and head in one position for a long time.
The load is small but continuous. The head often drifts forward toward the screen. This posture is
associated with neck pain in adults, but it has not been shown to cause it. For office neck and
shoulder pain, strength exercises for these muscles have the best evidence.

### What the app does

- Micro-breaks rotate through neck exercises: slow tilts and turns in a single plane.
  No neck circles.
- Optional: a daily 2-minute neck and shoulder strength routine following the Andersen protocol
  (for example shrugs or lateral raises with an elastic band).
- A gentle "change posture" cue every 30 minutes.

### What studies show

- **Chen et al., 2018, Physical Therapy 98(1):40-62.** Systematic review of 27 randomised trials
  in office workers. Neck and shoulder strengthening reduced pain: SMD 0.59 (0.29 to 0.89),
  moderate quality. General fitness: 0.43. Workstation ergonomics: low quality evidence.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29088401/
- **Andersen et al., 2008, Journal of Applied Physiology.** 42 women with trapezius myalgia.
  Specific strength training cut pain by 42-49%, cycling did not.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18948442/
- **Andersen et al., 2011, Pain 152(2):440-446.** 198 working adults. 2 minutes a day, 5 days a week with an
  elastic band reduced neck and shoulder pain almost as much as 12 minutes.
  doi:10.1016/j.pain.2010.11.016
- **Andersen et al., 2011, Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health.** Secondary analysis
  of the same trial: headache frequency fell by 43% (2 minutes a day) and 56% (12 minutes).
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21617837/
- **Mahmoud et al., 2019, Current Reviews in Musculoskeletal Medicine.** Forward head posture is
  associated with neck pain in adults. Causality is not shown. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31773477/
- **On neck circles.** There is no direct evidence of harm. Physiotherapists usually advise
  single-plane movements and we follow that advice. This is practitioner opinion, not a study result.

### When to see a doctor

- Pain or numbness spreading into the arm.
- Arm weakness.
- Neck pain after an injury, fall or accident.
- Neck pain with fever, dizziness, vision or speech problems: this is urgent.
- Pain that wakes you at night.
- Pain lasting longer than 6 weeks.

## Back and long sitting

### Why

Long uninterrupted sitting is harmful not because we "sit wrong" but because we move too little.
Moving every half hour to hour helps muscles and metabolism: blood sugar rises less after meals.
No posture has been found that protects against back pain. As physiotherapists put it, the best
posture is the next posture: changing it is what helps.

### What the app does

- Every 45 minutes (30, 45 or 60, your choice) it suggests standing up for 3 minutes: back and
  leg exercises, a short walk, a glass of water.
- A gentle "change posture" cue every 30 minutes.
- Statistics show your time at the computer and your longest sitting stretch.

### What studies show

- **Ekelund et al., 2016, The Lancet.** Meta-analysis of more than 1 million people. 60-75 minutes
  a day of moderate activity eliminated the excess mortality linked to high sitting time.
  https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(16)30370-1/abstract
- **Bull et al., 2020, British Journal of Sports Medicine: WHO guidelines.** Adults: 150-300 minutes
  of moderate activity a week and less sedentary time. WHO does not name a specific break
  frequency. doi:10.1136/bjsports-2020-102955
- **Dunstan et al., 2012, Diabetes Care 35(5):976-983.** 2 minutes of walking every 20 minutes
  after a meal: postprandial glucose down 24% (light walking) and 29.6% (moderate), insulin down 23%.
  doi:10.2337/dc11-1931
- **Duran, ..., Diaz, 2023, Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise 55(5):847-855.** 11 participants.
  5 minutes of walking every 30 minutes: glucose spikes down 58%, blood pressure down 4-5 mmHg,
  better mood. Small study. doi:10.1249/MSS.0000000000003109
- **Shrestha et al., 2018, Cochrane Review.** Sit-stand desks cut sitting by 84-116 minutes a day.
  Low quality evidence, health effects unclear. doi:10.1002/14651858.CD010912.pub5
- **Swain et al., 2020, Journal of Biomechanics 102:109312.** Systematic review: no consensus that
  spinal posture causes low back pain. doi:10.1016/j.jbiomech.2019.08.006
- **O'Sullivan et al., 2020, British Journal of Sports Medicine.** "10 facts about back pain":
  posture is not the cause, the best posture is the next posture.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31892534/

### When to see a doctor

Urgently, if back pain comes with:

- numbness in the groin, buttocks or inner thighs ("saddle" area);
- problems with bladder or bowel control;
- leg weakness that is getting worse.

Also see a doctor if the pain started after an injury, if you have fever, unexplained weight
loss, a history of cancer, severe night pain, or pain lasting longer than 4-6 weeks.

## Hands

### Why

Mouse and keyboard keep the hands and forearms in one position for hours, which can cause fatigue
and discomfort in the forearms. A link between computer use and carpal tunnel syndrome has not
been established: in studies, heavy computer users do not get it noticeably more often than
other people, and the difference is within chance. So we do not
claim the exercises protect against it. They are there to let the hands rest and change position.

### What the app does

- Some micro-breaks include simple hand and finger movements.
- The breaks themselves come every 20 minutes: studies show this reduces forearm discomfort.

### What studies show

- **Thomsen, Gerr & Atroshi, 2008, BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders.** Systematic review: evidence
  that computer work causes carpal tunnel syndrome is insufficient.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18838001/
- **Mediouni et al., 2014, Journal of Occupational and Environmental Medicine.** Meta-analysis of
  6 studies: a link between computer use and carpal tunnel syndrome could not be shown (odds ratio
  1.67, 95% CI 0.79 to 3.55, not significant). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24451617/
- **Page et al., 2012, Cochrane Review CD009899.** Exercises for carpal tunnel syndrome: weak
  evidence. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22696387/
- **Kim, 2015, Journal of Physical Therapy Science.** Review of exercises for carpal tunnel
  syndrome, also with cautious conclusions. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26357452/
- **McLean et al., 2001, Applied Ergonomics.** Micro-breaks every 20 minutes reduced discomfort in
  the neck, back and forearms without loss of productivity. This worked better than breaks every
  40 minutes or at will. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11394463/
- **Galinsky et al., 2000, Ergonomics.** Extra short breaks reduced discomfort and eye strain
  without loss of productivity. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/001401300184297
- **Galinsky et al., 2007, American Journal of Industrial Medicine.** Four extra 5-minute breaks
  reduced discomfort and eye strain without loss of productivity. Stretching on top added little.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17514726/

### When to see a doctor

- Numbness or tingling in the thumb, index and middle fingers, especially at night.
- Thumb weakness, dropping things.
- Symptoms lasting longer than 2-4 weeks.
- Pain after an injury.

## Legs

### Why

When we walk, the calf muscles contract and help push blood up the veins to the heart. When we sit,
this pump is almost off. By evening the legs feel swollen and heavy. Very long sitting without
breaks has been linked to blood clots, but in healthy young people this risk is low, and it has
not been directly shown that office breaks prevent clots. Breaks do reduce swelling and heaviness.

### What the app does

- The stand-up break every 45 minutes suggests getting up, walking and doing leg exercises such
  as calf raises.
- Statistics show your longest stretch without standing up.

### What studies show

- **Healy et al., 2010, Journal of the Royal Society of Medicine 103:447.** Case-control study:
  people who sat 10 or more hours a day and at least 2 hours without getting up had 2.8 times
  the odds of venous thromboembolism (95% CI 1.2 to 6.1). doi:10.1258/jrsm.2010.100155
- **Braithwaite et al., 2016, JRSM Open.** A second, larger study by the same group, 200 cases and
  200 controls. The same definition of long sitting was not significantly linked to clots (odds
  ratio 1.18), but each extra hour of average daily sitting raised the odds by about 8% (1.08).
  A weak association. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27540486/
- **Peddie et al., 2021, PLOS ONE.** Randomised crossover trial, 18 adults. 2-minute walks every
  30 minutes increased blood flow in the popliteal artery by about 80% compared with sitting;
  standing still helped only briefly. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7781669/

### When to see a doctor

Call emergency services if:

- one leg is swollen, painful, red or warm;
- you suddenly feel short of breath or have chest pain.

If you have had blood clots, use hormonal contraception, are pregnant, are being treated for
cancer or recently had surgery, talk to your doctor about how often you should get up and
whether you need anything else.

## Water

### Why

When the body loses noticeably more water than it gets, attention drops, mood worsens and a
headache may follow. At work, people often forget to drink. At the same time, "8 glasses a day"
is not a scientific norm: we get water from food and from any drink, including tea and coffee.

### What the app does

- Every 90 minutes it shows a small "drink some water" card in the corner of the screen.
  No window opens.
- The reminder can be turned off.

### What studies show

- **Wittbrodt & Millard-Stafford, 2018, Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise.** Meta-analysis of
  33 studies. Dehydration impairs cognitive performance: overall effect size -0.21, attention -0.52.
  The effect is stronger above 2% body mass loss.
  https://journals.lww.com/acsm-msse/Fulltext/2018/11000/Dehydration_Impairs_Cognitive_Performance__A.21.aspx
- **Armstrong et al., 2012, Journal of Nutrition.** 1.36% body mass loss in women: worse mood and
  concentration, more headaches. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22190027/
- **Ganio et al., 2011, British Journal of Nutrition.** 1.59% loss in men: worse vigilance and
  working memory. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21736786/
- In all these studies dehydration was induced by exercise, heat or diuretics, and samples were
  small. How well this applies to an ordinary office day is unclear.
- **EFSA, 2010.** Adequate total water intake: 2.0 L a day for women, 2.5 L for men. This includes
  water from food (about 20-30%) and all drinks. doi:10.2903/j.efsa.2010.1459
- **Valtin, 2002, American Journal of Physiology.** Review: the "8 glasses a day" rule is not
  based on evidence. https://geiselmed.dartmouth.edu/news/2002_h2/08aug2002_water.shtml

### When to see a doctor

- Do not force yourself to drink beyond thirst: too much water in a short time is dangerous
  (blood sodium drops).
- If you have heart or kidney failure and your doctor has limited your fluids, follow your doctor
  and turn the water reminder off in settings.
- Constant strong thirst, frequent urination, dry mouth for no reason: a reason to have your blood
  sugar checked.

## Breathing

### Why

Slow breathing, around 6 breaths a minute, strengthens the vagal influence on the heart: heart
rate variability goes up. Many people feel calmer after a few minutes of it.

### What the app does

- Optional breathing pause: breathe in for 4 seconds, out for 6 seconds, about 6 breaths a minute.
  No breath holds, no forced breathing.

### What studies show

- **Zaccaro et al., 2018, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience.** Systematic review: slow breathing is
  associated with changes in autonomic and brain activity and with feeling calmer.
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6137615/
- **Laborde et al., 2022, Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews.** Meta-analysis of 223 studies:
  slow breathing raises vagally mediated heart rate variability.
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0149763422002007
- **Fincham et al., 2023, Scientific Reports.** Meta-analysis of 12 randomised trials, 785 people:
  breathwork reduced stress, g = -0.35. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-27247-y
- **Balban et al., 2023, Cell Reports Medicine.** 5 minutes a day: cyclic sighing (long exhales)
  improved mood more than mindfulness meditation. A single study run remotely.
  https://www.cell.com/cell-reports-medicine/fulltext/S2666-3791(22)00474-8

### When to see a doctor

- Dizziness or tingling in the fingers or lips: go back to normal breathing.
- Breathing exercises do not treat anxiety disorders or panic attacks. If anxiety gets in the
  way of your life, see a doctor or therapist.
- Shortness of breath, chest pain, choking attacks: see a doctor, urgently if it starts suddenly.

## Workstation

### Why

A good workstation does not make long sitting harmless, but it removes extra strain: no craning
toward the screen, no bent wrists, no squinting at glare.

### What the app does

- The app does not check your workstation. The tips below are worth doing once.

### What is recommended

Based on OSHA (US Occupational Safety and Health Administration) guidance:

- Top of the screen at or slightly below eye level.
- Screen at arm's length, 50-100 cm.
- Elbows at about 90–120°, wrists straight.
- Feet flat on the floor or on a footrest.
- Lower back supported by the backrest.
- 2-3 fingers' width between the seat edge and the back of the knee.
- Lighting without glare on the screen.

### What studies show

- **OSHA Computer Workstations eTool.** https://www.osha.gov/etools/computer-workstations ;
  monitors: https://www.osha.gov/etools/computer-workstations/components/monitors
- **Hoe et al., 2018, Cochrane Review.** Extra breaks may reduce discomfort (very low quality evidence).
  Arm supports and alternative mice gave mixed results. Workstation adjustment, sit-stand desks
  and ergonomics training showed no effect. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30350850/

So setting up your workstation is worth it for comfort, but moving and taking breaks matter more.

### When to see a doctor

If discomfort continues after adjusting your workstation and taking regular breaks, see the
sections on eyes, neck, back and hands.

## Micro-breaks in general

### Why

During a short break both the muscles and attention get to rest.

### What the app does

- Short breaks every 20 minutes, longer ones every 45 minutes.
- Does not interrupt calls: it only checks whether another program is using the camera or
  microphone and never opens them itself. Waits while you are in a fullscreen app, respects
  system Do Not Disturb where the system lets it be read.
- Focus mode for 25 or 50 minutes, and work hours.

### What studies show

- **Albulescu et al., 2022, PLOS ONE.** Meta-analysis of micro-breaks: more vigour (d = 0.36),
  less fatigue (d = 0.35). Overall effect on performance not significant (d = 0.16). Longer breaks
  had bigger effects. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36044424/
- **McLean et al., 2001** and **Galinsky et al., 2000, 2007**: see "Hands".

### When to see a doctor

Constant fatigue that does not go away after rest and weekends is a reason to see a GP.

## End of the day

### Why

If you manage to stop thinking about work after work, rest and sleep are better. A short
end-of-day ritual helps you switch off.

### What the app does

- Optionally reminds you to finish work at the end of your working day.
- Once a week asks you to rate how your eyes, neck, back and hands feel, from 0 to 3.
  Answers stay on your computer.

### What studies show

- **Wendsche & Lohmann-Haislah, 2017, Frontiers in Psychology.** Meta-analysis: detaching from work
  in free time is linked to better wellbeing and sleep and less burnout. These are correlations,
  not proof of cause. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28133454/
- **Boubekri et al., 2014, Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine.** Pilot study, 49 people: those who
  worked near windows slept about 46 minutes longer. Weak data, but getting some daylight will not
  hurt. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24932139/

### When to see a doctor

If you have slept badly for more than three weeks or constantly lack energy or mood, see a doctor.
